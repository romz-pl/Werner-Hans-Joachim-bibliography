# Hans-Joachim Werner Bibliography

Hans-Joachim Werner is a German theoretical chemist, born in Hamburg in 1950, and one of the most internationally recognized figures in quantum chemistry. He is now retired. The Institute for Theoretical Chemistry at the University of Stuttgart lists him as a retired group leader, and Andreas Köhn is currently head of the institute.

He studied chemistry at the University of Mainz, earned his PhD in 1977 at the Max Planck Institute for Biophysical Chemistry in Göttingen, and then worked in Cambridge and at the University of Frankfurt, where he completed his habilitation in 1982. He became a full professor at the University of Bielefeld in 1987 and moved to Stuttgart in 1994 as professor and director of the Institute for Theoretical Chemistry. He also served as dean of chemistry at Bielefeld and later at Stuttgart, and as vice rector for organization and research at Stuttgart from 2012.

His best-known contribution is the Molpro quantum chemistry program, which he developed as principal author. It is used by hundreds of research groups worldwide. His methods work focuses on accurate electronic structure calculations for large molecules, particularly local electron correlation methods, which make high-level calculations feasible for bigger systems. His group applied these methods to reaction mechanisms, spectroscopy, and non-covalent interactions.

His honors include the Leibniz Prize from the German Research Foundation (2000), an ERC Advanced Grant (2013), the Erich Hückel Prize from the German Chemical Society (2018), and a Humboldt Research Award (1988). He has also sat on the editorial boards of several theoretical and computational chemistry journals.
